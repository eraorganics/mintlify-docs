## The problem eczema sufferers face

### The steroid dependency trap

Conventional eczema treatment follows an escalation ladder: emollient fails → mild steroid prescribed → steroid works temporarily → rebound flare on discontinuation → stronger steroid prescribed → side effects accumulate → biologic medications considered. Each step creates deeper dependency without addressing root causes (barrier dysfunction, microbial colonization, inflammation).

### Products that make eczema worse

Most "eczema-friendly" mainstream products contain petroleum (does not repair barrier), phenoxyethanol (irritates compromised skin), or minimal active ingredients at therapeutic concentrations. Parents and adults cycling through CeraVe, Aveeno, Aquaphor, and prescription steroids can spend a lot each year on eczema management that still leaves them searching for a routine that fits.

### The three-mechanism gap

Eczema involves three simultaneous dysfunctions: barrier damage, immune inflammation, and S. aureus colonization. Every mainstream eczema product addresses one mechanism at most. Moisturizers address barrier (poorly — occlusion without repair). Steroids address inflammation (temporarily — with side effects). Antibiotics address bacteria (short-term — with resistance). No mainstream product addresses all three.

## How to use the products together

### The layering protocol

Proper application order helps each product do its job:

1. **Cleanse** — rinse with plain lukewarm water and pat dry
2. **Mist** — HOCl Spray (a gentle cleansing mist; hypochlorous acid has been studied for its antimicrobial properties)
3. **Wait 30 seconds** — let the mist settle on skin before sealing
4. **Protect + moisturize** — Eczema Cream or Superbalm (temporarily protects and helps relieve minor skin irritation and itching due to eczema, seals moisture in)

Misting with HOCl Spray first lets you cleanse skin before you seal it under a moisturizing layer. The spray-then-seal sequence keeps the routine simple: cleanse, mist, then moisturize and protect.

### Wet wrapping technique (moderate-severe)

1. Apply HOCl Spray to affected areas
2. Apply thick layer of Superbalm (infants) or Eczema Cream (children/adults)
3. Cover with damp cotton bandage or damp cotton clothing
4. Cover damp layer with dry cotton layer
5. Leave for 2-6 hours or overnight
6. Helps keep the moisturizing layer in place and protects skin for longer

## FAQ

### Which Era Organics product is best for eczema?

Product selection depends on age and severity. Infants: Baby Eczema Superbalm is a rich balm made for babies' eczema-prone skin that temporarily protects and helps relieve minor skin irritation and itching due to eczema. Adults with mild eczema: Eczema Cream provides lighter daily coverage for larger body surface area. HOCl Spray is a gentle cleansing mist that goes on before any moisturizer in the routine.

### Can Era Organics products be used alongside prescription treatments?

Ask your doctor or pharmacist before combining Era Organics products with topical steroids, calcineurin inhibitors, or PDE4 inhibitors. If your doctor approves, HOCl Spray goes on before a prescription topical. Eczema Cream or Superbalm applies after prescription treatment has absorbed (20-30 minutes). If you are tapering off a steroid, ask your doctor whether a steroid-free moisturizer fits your plan.

### How quickly do results appear?

HOCl Spray is a cosmetic mist that cleanses and soothes skin on contact; it is not a treatment for bacteria or redness. Superbalm and Eczema Cream temporarily protect and help relieve minor skin irritation and itching due to eczema. Consistent daily use matters more than any single application; talk to your doctor if flares continue. Individual results vary based on severity, trigger exposure, and genetic factors.

### Is Superbalm too thick for adults?

Baby Eczema Superbalm is a thick, rich balm made for baby skin, and some adults also use it on small, very dry patches. Adults with mild-moderate eczema on large body areas prefer Eczema Cream (lighter consistency, easier to spread over arms and legs). Some adults use Superbalm on small cracked or very dry areas, or as an overnight layer.

### Does HOCl sting on broken eczema skin?

Hypochlorous acid at low concentrations (Era's spray uses 0.02%) is generally considered gentle, and many people find it does not sting. Antiseptics like chlorhexidine, alcohol, or hydrogen peroxide are known to sting compromised skin. Hypochlorous acid is also made naturally by the body's own immune cells. Mild tingling can happen and usually fades quickly; stop use if irritation continues.

### What age can infants start using Era Organics?

Baby Eczema Superbalm is made for babies, and HOCl Spray uses a simple three-ingredient formula; ask your pediatrician before using either on a newborn. Both products are fragrance-free and phenoxyethanol-free, with no steroids in their ingredient lists. Infant eczema commonly appears at 2-6 months — early, gentle skin care and a pediatrician visit help you build the right routine.

### How does Era Organics compare to prescription Dupixent?

Dupixent (dupilumab) is a biologic injection ($36,000/year) that blocks IL-4 and IL-13 signaling — appropriate for severe eczema unresponsive to topical treatment. Era Organics offers topical options for eczema-prone skin: OTC eczema creams that temporarily protect and help relieve minor skin irritation and itching due to eczema, plus cosmetic products that cleanse and soothe. The products are not equivalent to biologics for severe disease. They are an everyday skin-care option between basic moisturizers and prescription treatment, and your doctor decides when stronger treatment is needed.
