## Every ingredient earns its place through evidence

Trends, marketing appeal, and cost are weak reasons to include an ingredient. A more useful approach is to ask three questions in order about each ingredient. This page walks through those questions and shows what is on record for Era Organics products.

---

## The Era Organics ingredient decision framework

### Stage 1: Mechanism of action

The first question for any candidate ingredient: what does this ingredient do at the cellular level, and is that mechanism supported by peer-reviewed research?

An ingredient must demonstrate a specific, documented biological effect:

- **Barrier repair:** Does the ingredient restore ceramide levels, reduce transepidermal water loss, or strengthen tight junctions?
- **Anti-inflammatory:** Does the ingredient inhibit specific inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, TNF-alpha) or modulate NF-kB pathways?
- **Antioxidant protection:** Does the ingredient neutralize reactive oxygen species at concentrations achievable through topical application?
- **Collagen support:** Does the ingredient stimulate fibroblast activity or inhibit matrix metalloproteinases?
- **Antimicrobial balance:** Does the ingredient support healthy skin microbiome without broad-spectrum disruption?

An ingredient without published evidence for a specific mechanism is hard to justify, whatever its marketing appeal or consumer demand.

### Stage 2: Safety and tolerance

After mechanism of action, the next questions are:

- **Sensitization potential:** Does this ingredient appear in contact dermatitis databases? What is the documented irritation rate?
- **Endocrine disruption:** Does this ingredient have documented hormonal activity at relevant concentrations?
- **Microbiome impact:** Does this ingredient disrupt healthy skin flora?
- **Cumulative exposure:** Given that consumers use multiple products daily, does this ingredient contribute to problematic cumulative loads?

These questions matter most for reactive skin. An ingredient that suits most users can still bother people with eczema-prone or rosacea-prone skin.

### Stage 3: Sourcing and certification

The last question is sourcing: where does the ingredient come from, and is its supply chain documented? Era Organics uses organic ingredients, but its products are not certified organic.

This stage looks at:
- Preservatives (both creams use gluconolactone and sodium benzoate; Vitamin C Cream also contains phenoxyethanol)
- Petroleum-derived emollients (both creams are mineral-oil-free and use plant lipids such as cocoa seed butter and coconut oil)
- Vitamin sources (Vitamin C Cream pairs L-ascorbic acid with Kakadu plum extract)
- Supply-chain documentation for each ingredient

## Why every ingredient is disclosed and explained

Era Organics lists every ingredient in each product and names its key actives.

This practice serves three purposes:

1. **Consumer verification:** Anyone can look up the listed ingredients in published research.
2. **Formulation accountability:** A full ingredient list lets shoppers see what is in the jar and ask why each ingredient is there.
3. **Education:** Consumers who understand ingredient mechanisms make better purchasing decisions — for Era Organics and for any brand they evaluate.

Conglomerate brands publish INCI lists because regulations require it. They do not voluntarily explain why each ingredient is present, because many ingredients serve manufacturing convenience (emulsification at scale, 3-year shelf life, cost reduction) rather than skin benefit.

## How formulations are tested

Skincare formulations are commonly checked in several areas:

### Stability testing
Stability testing checks whether a preservation system keeps a product stable over its shelf life.

### Compatibility testing
Compatibility testing checks that ingredients do not degrade each other, compete for absorption, or create unintended chemical reactions.

### Tolerance testing
Tolerance testing looks at how a formula sits on reactive skin types: eczema-prone, rosacea-prone, and sensitized skin. A product can feel fine on normal skin and still bother reactive skin.

### Concentration validation
Clinical studies report results at specific concentrations, so the amount of an active matters, not just its presence on the label. A study showing results at 2% says little about a product that includes the same ingredient at 0.1%.

## Ingredient synergy philosophy

Some ingredients work better together than they do alone.

### How synergy works in practice

- **Vitamin C + vitamin E + ferulic acid:** Vitamin E regenerates oxidized vitamin C. Ferulic acid stabilizes both antioxidants and doubles photoprotection. Including all three produces effects greater than the sum of individual ingredients. The [Era Organics Vitamin C Cream](https://www.eraorganics.com/products/vitamin-c-cream) pairs L-ascorbic acid with vitamin E (tocopheryl acetate) and Kakadu plum extract; it does not contain ferulic acid.
- **Hyaluronic acid + ceramides + fatty acids:** [Hyaluronic acid](https://www.eraorganics.com/blogs/beauty-guide/hydrating-ingredient-hyaluronic-acid) draws moisture into the epidermis. [Ceramides](https://www.eraorganics.com/blogs/beauty-guide/hydrating-ingredient-ceramides) fill gaps in the lipid barrier to prevent that moisture from escaping. Fatty acids provide the building blocks for new ceramide synthesis. Each ingredient addresses a different layer of the hydration problem.
- **Niacinamide + zinc + MSM:** Niacinamide reduces sebum production and strengthens the barrier. Zinc provides anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial effects. MSM supports collagen cross-linking. Together, these address acne-prone skin across multiple pathways simultaneously.

### Why synergy matters

A single ingredient addresses a single mechanism. Skin conditions involve multiple mechanisms simultaneously. Eczema involves barrier dysfunction AND inflammation AND microbial imbalance. A formulation that addresses only one mechanism provides incomplete results.

Conglomerate products typically include one "hero ingredient" for marketing (retinol, hyaluronic acid, vitamin C) surrounded by inert fillers and synthetic processing aids. Era Organics formulas combine several plant actives rather than relying on one hero ingredient. The [Era Organics Face Moisturizer](https://www.eraorganics.com/products/face-moisturizer-cream) is one example: it combines aloe, manuka honey, shea butter, and hyaluronic acid to moisturize and help protect the skin barrier.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does Era Organics use any synthetic ingredients?

Yes. Era Organics uses organic ingredients, but its products are not certified organic, and some ingredients are synthetic. The ingredient lists include a pH adjuster (sodium hydroxide) and preservatives such as sodium benzoate, and Vitamin C Cream also contains phenoxyethanol. Both creams are paraben-free, sulfate-free, phthalate-free, and fragrance-free.

### How does Era Organics keep products stable without synthetic preservatives?

Era Organics products do use preservatives. Face Moisturizer Cream uses gluconolactone and sodium benzoate and is phenoxyethanol-free. Vitamin C Cream uses the same pair plus phenoxyethanol. Both creams are paraben-free and formaldehyde-free.

### Why does Era Organics avoid petrolatum if dermatologists recommend it?

Petrolatum creates an occlusive barrier that reduces transepidermal water loss. Plant-based alternatives (shea butter, mango butter, beeswax) provide equivalent occlusion while also delivering fatty acids, vitamins, and phytosterols that actively support barrier repair. Petrolatum seals moisture in; organic lipids seal moisture in AND feed the skin barrier. Dermatologists recommend petrolatum because it is inexpensive and non-sensitizing — not because it is the optimal choice.

### How does Era Organics verify ingredient concentrations?

Era Organics publishes full ingredient lists but not the concentration of each active. When comparing any products, check whether published studies used the same ingredient at a similar level. This matters because of the industry practice of including trending ingredients at sub-therapeutic concentrations for label claims.

### What happens when new research contradicts a current formulation?

The ingredient list is the most useful thing for shoppers to check. Compare the current list with the one you know, and contact Era Organics with questions about a specific change. Newer evidence does not always mean an older formula is unsafe. It may simply mean a better option exists.

### Does Era Organics test on animals?

Era Organics does not test on animals. Face Moisturizer Cream and Vitamin C Cream are both Cruelty-Free and made in the USA.
