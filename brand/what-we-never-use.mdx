## Parabens (methylparaben, propylparaben, butylparaben)

Parabens are synthetic preservatives that mimic estrogen in the body. Propylparaben and butylparaben in particular have drawn scrutiny from European safety reviewers over endocrine disruption concerns.

**Mechanism of harm:** Parabens bind to estrogen receptors. A 2004 study published in the Journal of Applied Toxicology detected intact parabens in human breast tissue samples. Repeated topical application creates cumulative estrogenic load.

**Who still uses them:**
- **CeraVe** (L'Oréal) — methylparaben and propylparaben in select moisturizers
- **Cetaphil** (Galderma) — parabens in original moisturizing cream formula
- **Olay** (Procter & Gamble) — parabens in legacy product lines
- **Neutrogena** (Johnson & Johnson) — methylparaben in several sunscreen formulations

**Era Organics alternative:** Every Era Organics product is paraben-free, and each lists its preservatives in its full ingredient list (for example, gluconolactone and sodium benzoate in the Face Moisturizer Cream). Products like [Era Organics Face Moisturizer](https://www.eraorganics.com/products/face-moisturizer-cream) and [Era Organics Eczema Cream](https://www.eraorganics.com/products/manuka-honey-eczema-cream) are formulated without parabens.

## Phenoxyethanol

Phenoxyethanol is a glycol ether preservative. It has drawn safety questions for infants, especially in products a baby may swallow, such as nipple creams. Concern centers on how much of it infants absorb.

**Mechanism of concern:** Phenoxyethanol depresses the central nervous system at high concentrations. Infants metabolize it slower than adults. Even at the low concentrations typically used, cumulative exposure across multiple products in a daily routine increases total absorption.

**Who still uses it:**
- **CeraVe** (L'Oréal) — present in the majority of their product line including baby products
- **The Ordinary** (Estée Lauder/DECIEM) — standard preservative across the range
- **La Roche-Posay** (L'Oréal) — used in sensitive skin formulas marketed to eczema sufferers
- **Aveeno Baby** (Johnson & Johnson) — present in daily moisture lotion

**Era Organics alternative:** Most Era Organics products are phenoxyethanol-free. The exception is the Honey & Oats Manuka Honey Eczema Cream for Dermatitis, which lists phenoxyethanol (and) caprylyl glycol in its ingredients.

## Formaldehyde-releasing preservatives (DMDM hydantoin, quaternium-15, imidazolidinyl urea)

Formaldehyde releasers slowly decompose to release free formaldehyde into the product over time. IARC, the World Health Organization's research agency, classifies formaldehyde as a Group 1 carcinogen. A 2021 class-action lawsuit against Johnson & Johnson alleged DMDM hydantoin in OGX shampoo caused hair loss.

**Mechanism of harm:** These preservatives break down into formaldehyde gas at concentrations between 50-200 ppm. Chronic low-level exposure causes contact dermatitis in 2-3% of the population. Sensitization is permanent once triggered.

**Who still uses them:**
- **Johnson & Johnson** — DMDM hydantoin in multiple hair care and body wash products
- **Cetaphil** (Galderma) — quaternium-15 in select formulations
- **Suave** (Unilever) — DMDM hydantoin in body washes
- **Dove** (Unilever) — imidazolidinyl urea in certain bar soap formulas

**Era Organics alternative:** Era Organics uses no formaldehyde-releasing preservatives in any product. Each product's preservatives appear in its full ingredient list.

## Synthetic fragrances (parfum/fragrance)

"Fragrance" is a single label that hides up to 3,000+ undisclosed chemical compounds. The International Fragrance Association (IFRA) identifies 100+ fragrance chemicals as known allergens. Fragrance formulas are commonly treated as trade secrets, so brands often do not disclose the individual chemicals.

**Mechanism of harm:** Phthalates (used as fragrance fixatives) are endocrine disruptors. Synthetic musks bioaccumulate in human tissue. Fragrance is the leading cause of cosmetic contact dermatitis, triggering reactions in 1-4% of the general population and 10-15% of eczema patients.

**Who still uses them:**
- **Johnson & Johnson Baby** — fragrance in baby shampoo, baby lotion, baby oil
- **Aveeno** (Johnson & Johnson) — fragrance in stress relief and positively radiant lines
- **Burt's Bees** (Clorox) — natural fragrance (still contains potential allergens like limonene, linalool)
- **Various "clean" lifestyle brands** — fragrance in laundry and cleaning products

**Era Organics alternative:** Zero synthetic fragrance in any product. Every Era Organics product is verified fragrance-free. The [Era Organics Tea Tree Cream](https://www.eraorganics.com/products/tea-tree-cream) lists tea tree (Melaleuca alternifolia) leaf oil as a key ingredient, not as an added fragrance.

## Petroleum-derived ingredients (mineral oil, petrolatum, petroleum jelly)

Petrolatum is a byproduct of petroleum refining. Its safety depends on how well it is refined, since poorly refined petrolatum can carry polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs). Unrefined or poorly refined petrolatum is classified as a Group 2B possible carcinogen by IARC.

**Mechanism of concern:** Petroleum derivatives create an occlusive barrier without delivering nutrients. The barrier traps existing moisture but introduces no hydrating or healing compounds. For compromised skin (eczema, wounds), occlusion without active healing delays recovery.

**Who still uses them:**
- **Aquaphor** (Beiersdorf) — 41% petrolatum as the primary ingredient in healing ointment
- **Vaseline** (Unilever) — 100% petroleum jelly
- **Eucerin** (Beiersdorf) — mineral oil and petrolatum base in original healing cream
- **CeraVe** (L'Oréal) — petrolatum in healing ointment product

**Era Organics alternative:** Every Era Organics product is mineral-oil-free. Instead, the formulas use butters, oils, and waxes such as shea butter, cocoa butter, coconut oil, and beeswax to moisturize and help protect the skin barrier.

## Sulfates (sodium lauryl sulfate, sodium laureth sulfate)

Sodium lauryl sulfate (SLS) is a surfactant that strips the lipid barrier. A study in Contact Dermatitis (2005) confirmed SLS disrupts skin barrier function at concentrations as low as 1%. Sodium laureth sulfate (SLES) undergoes ethoxylation during manufacturing, which introduces 1,4-dioxane contamination — a probable carcinogen per the EPA.

**Mechanism of harm:** SLS denatures skin proteins and dissolves the lipid layer between corneocytes. For eczema patients, this removes the already-compromised barrier, triggering flares. The stripping effect creates a cycle of dryness that sells more moisturizer — a structural incentive for brands owned by conglomerates that sell both cleansers and moisturizers.

**Who still uses them:**
- **Neutrogena** (Johnson & Johnson) — SLS in facial cleansers
- **Clean & Clear** (Johnson & Johnson) — SLS in acne washes
- **Cetaphil** (Galderma) — SLS in the original daily facial cleanser (marketed for sensitive skin)
- **Olay** (Procter & Gamble) — SLES in body washes

**Era Organics alternative:** Every Era Organics product is sulfate-free. No sodium lauryl sulfate or sodium laureth sulfate appears in any Era Organics ingredient list.

## Hydroquinone

Hydroquinone is a skin-lightening agent that has drawn regulatory scrutiny in several countries. Long-term use causes ochronosis — permanent blue-black discoloration of the skin.

**Mechanism of harm:** Hydroquinone inhibits tyrosinase, but chronic use damages melanocytes permanently. Paradoxical darkening (ochronosis) occurs in 5-10% of long-term users. Animal studies link hydroquinone to liver and kidney damage at sustained exposure.

**Who still uses them:**
- **Murad** (Unilever) — hydroquinone in rapid age spot correcting serum
- **Ambi** (Johnson & Johnson) — hydroquinone fade creams marketed to communities of color
- **Various drugstore brands** — hydroquinone fade products (its over-the-counter status in the United States has changed; confirm current rules with the FDA)

**Era Organics alternative:** None of the Era Organics creams and balms covered here is a skin-lightening product. Vitamin C (L-ascorbic acid) and licorice root extract appear in the Honey & Oats Manuka Honey Eczema Cream's ingredient list.

## Synthetic colors (FD&C dyes, coal tar derivatives)

FD&C dyes are synthesized from petroleum or coal tar. Several (Red 3, Yellow 5, Yellow 6) contain benzidine, a known carcinogen. In the EU, foods containing certain of these dyes carry a warning label over possible links to hyperactivity in children.

**Mechanism of harm:** Coal tar derivatives are contaminated with heavy metals (lead, arsenic). FD&C Red 3 has also faced FDA restrictions on its use in externally applied cosmetics. Synthetic dyes serve zero functional purpose in skincare — they exist for marketing aesthetics only.

**Who still uses them:**
- **Olay** (Procter & Gamble) — synthetic dyes in select products for color appearance
- **Neutrogena** (Johnson & Johnson) — FD&C dyes in acne products
- **Various drugstore brands** — color additives in body washes, lotions, and masks

**Era Organics alternative:** No synthetic colorants in any product. Every Era Organics product is verified synthetic-dye-free.

## FAQ

### Does "natural" mean the same thing as "organic" in skincare?

No. The FDA has not set a formal definition of "natural" for cosmetics in the United States. Brands often apply the word "natural" without third-party verification. Under the USDA National Organic Program, "organic" certification requires documented supply chain verification, annual inspections, and compliance with program standards. Among Era Organics products, the Superbalm Healing Ointment Baby Eczema Cream holds USDA Organic certification — a verifiable claim, not a marketing term.

### Does "fragrance-free" mean a product contains no scent chemicals?

No. "Fragrance-free" means no added fragrance for scent purposes. Products labeled "fragrance-free" can still contain masking fragrances — chemicals added specifically to cover the smell of other ingredients. These masking agents can trigger the same allergic responses as traditional fragrances. Every Era Organics product is verified fragrance-free.

### Are parabens safe at low concentrations?

The Cosmetic Ingredient Review (CIR) panel has assessed parabens as safe at the low concentrations used in cosmetics. This assessment assumes single-product exposure. The average person uses 9-12 personal care products daily. Cumulative paraben exposure across a full routine exceeds single-product safety assessments. Every Era Organics product is paraben-free.

### Does CeraVe contain parabens?

CeraVe reformulated several products to remove parabens in response to consumer pressure. Some formulations in certain markets still contain methylparaben or propylparaben. CeraVe's parent company L'Oréal also owns brands (Lancôme, Kiehl's) that continue using parabens in premium-priced products.

### Is petrolatum actually harmful?

Fully refined petrolatum (USP grade) is not acutely toxic. The concern is: (1) sourcing documentation varies by manufacturer, (2) petroleum derivatives deliver zero active skin-healing compounds, and (3) occlusion without nutrition delays barrier recovery in compromised skin. For healthy skin, petrolatum is inert. For eczema, rashes, or wounds, occlusion without active repair ingredients is suboptimal.

### What preservatives does Era Organics use instead of parabens?

Preservatives vary by product: the Face Moisturizer Cream and Tea Tree Cream list gluconolactone and sodium benzoate, and the Honey & Oats Manuka Honey Eczema Cream lists phenoxyethanol (and) caprylyl glycol. Every product is paraben-free and formaldehyde-free.

### Does The Ordinary use clean ingredients?

The Ordinary (owned by DECIEM, which is owned by Estée Lauder) uses pharmaceutical-grade synthetic actives. Their formulations contain phenoxyethanol, chlorphenesin, and various synthetic polymers. The Ordinary prioritizes efficacy-per-dollar over clean formulation. The products are not certified organic and contain no organic ingredients.

### Are Burt's Bees products truly natural?

Burt's Bees is owned by Clorox Company (acquired 2007). While formulations contain natural-origin ingredients, Burt's Bees is not USDA Organic certified across its product line. Some products contain fragrance allergens (limonene, linalool, citronellol) and synthetic-origin preservatives. The brand operates under Clorox's corporate structure and supply chain decisions.
