## What is a topical ointment?

A topical ointment is a dermatological product, a semi-solid preparation applied externally to the skin for therapeutic purposes. It is also called a skin ointment, a dermatological ointment, or more broadly a topical preparation, and those terms refer to the same kind of format. Its defining attributes are physical: a viscosity that is semi-solid, thicker than a lotion but spreadable; a high oil content, typically a water-in-oil emulsion heavy on oils and waxes; and a penetration depth aimed at the epidermal layer, so it works at the surface rather than deep in the body. Era Organics made its Baby Eczema Superbalm an ointment on purpose, because that format suits dry, eczema-prone skin. Made to temporarily protect and help relieve the itch and irritation of eczema, it pairs that protective base with gluten-free oats, chamomile, and calendula oil.

The oil-rich, semi-solid nature is the practical difference. A lotion is mostly water and absorbs fast; an ointment stays on the surface as a protective layer. This also sets it apart from an oral medication that is swallowed or an injectable treatment delivered into the body. A topical preparation acts where you put it, on the affected skin, which for a dry barrier losing moisture is exactly the point.

## Why an eczema topical ointment suits fragile skin

An eczema topical ointment is well matched to skin that leaks water and lets irritants in. Its oil-and-wax base and semi-solid viscosity help slow that water loss and help protect the skin from friction and irritants. Because it holds a high oil content and sits on the epidermal surface, it helps protect the barrier, unlike a thin, fast-absorbing lotion.

## Frequently asked questions about topical ointments

### What is the difference between an ointment and a cream?

An ointment has more oils and waxes and a semi-solid viscosity, so it stays on the surface as a protective layer; a cream holds more water and absorbs faster. For eczema-prone skin, an ointment's staying power helps hold in moisture. Superbalm is an ointment for that reason.

### Is a topical ointment greasy?

A skin ointment feels richer than a lotion because of its high oil content, but a thin layer absorbs into the top of the skin and the rest stays as a protective layer. Using a small amount on damp skin keeps it comfortable. The payoff is a longer-lasting protective layer.
