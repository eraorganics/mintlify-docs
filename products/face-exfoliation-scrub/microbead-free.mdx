## What does microbead-free mean?

Microbead-free means a scrub uses biodegradable, naturally sourced grit to buff the skin instead of the tiny plastic beads that older exfoliants relied on. In the language of cosmetic formulation, microbead-free is a type of formulation attribute — a statement about what a product leaves out and what it uses in place of it. The same idea appears on labels under a few names — as a plastic-free exfoliant, as no microbeads, or as a biodegradable scrub — but each describes the same choice: natural abrasive grit rather than synthetic polymer spheres. Era Organics built its Revive+ scrub on plant-derived grit, so it exfoliates without any plastic beads.

The attribute breaks down into three component parts. There is the natural abrasive itself — the grit that does the buffing. There is the plain absence of plastic beads, no polyethylene spheres anywhere in the formula. And there is the biodegradable grit that replaces them, material that breaks down in the environment rather than persisting. Those three together are what let a scrub deliver tactile smoothing without the synthetic microplastic that gave the old scrubs their texture.

## What plastic microbeads were, and why they were phased out

Plastic microbeads were tiny, perfectly round spheres of polyethylene or polypropylene added to scrubs, cleansers, and toothpastes to provide cheap, uniform abrasion. Their problem was not on the skin but afterward. Because they are small, solid, and non-biodegradable, they slip through wastewater filters and end up in rivers, lakes, and oceans, where they accumulate and are eaten by aquatic life. That environmental impact led many places to restrict or ban plastic microbeads in rinse-off cosmetics. The bead that felt smooth in the shower turned out to be a persistent piece of microplastic downstream.

This is the antonym at the heart of the attribute. Polyethylene beads and plastic microbeads are exactly what a microbead-free scrub is defined against. Removing them is not a cosmetic downgrade — natural grit can buff the surface much as a bead did — it is an environmental upgrade, swapping a persistent synthetic for a natural material.

Three attributes define a good microbead-free design. The abrasive source should be natural and renewable rather than petroleum-derived plastic. ## Frequently asked questions about microbead-free scrubs

### Why are plastic microbeads a problem?

Plastic microbeads are tiny, non-biodegradable spheres that slip through water treatment and accumulate in rivers and oceans as microplastic, where aquatic life ingests them. That environmental harm led many places to restrict them in rinse-off products. A microbead-free scrub avoids the problem entirely by using biodegradable natural grit.

### What replaces microbeads in a natural scrub?

Naturally sourced abrasives do the buffing instead. In the Revive+ formula that is walnut shell powder — a plant-derived grit. It buffs the skin by friction the way a bead did, and it is not plastic.

### Does microbead-free mean less effective exfoliation?

Not necessarily. Natural grit can buff the surface much as a plastic bead did. Microbead-free changes the material the scrub uses. Whatever the grit, light pressure keeps the buff gentle on the skin.

### Is a biodegradable scrub better for the environment?

Yes, in the sense that its abrasive breaks down after use instead of persisting as microplastic. Swapping plastic beads for biodegradable grit removes a lasting downstream cost while keeping a similar smoothing action, which is why it is considered the more responsible choice.
