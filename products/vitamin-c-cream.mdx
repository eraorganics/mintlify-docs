## Era Organics Vitamin C Cream is a moisturizer that pairs vitamin C with supporting antioxidants for skin that looks dull or uneven in tone.

The formula combines L-ascorbic acid with vitamin E and botanical antioxidants, including Kakadu plum and green tea extract, in a moisturizing cream base. It is made for people who want vitamin C in a moisturizing cream rather than a bare, highly acidic serum.

---

## Why Era Organics formulated this product

Vitamin C is the most evidence-backed topical antioxidant in dermatology. Clinical literature has studied its role in brightening the look of skin, collagen synthesis, neutralizing free radicals, and the visible signs of photoaging. The problem: many vitamin C products fall short in one of three ways:

1. **Unstable formulations** — pure L-ascorbic acid (the active form) can oxidize after opening, turning brown and generating free radicals rather than neutralizing them. Some vitamin C products can oxidize before they are used up.
2. **Irritating concentrations** — clinical serums (SkinCeuticals CE Ferulic at 15% L-ascorbic acid, pH 2.5) can cause stinging, redness, and peeling in sensitive skin types. Efficacy can come at the cost of tolerability.
3. **Incomplete antioxidant systems** — vitamin C alone is consumed by free radicals and becomes inactive. Without vitamin E to regenerate it and ferulic acid to stabilize both, vitamin C delivers a fraction of its potential.

Era Organics Vitamin C Cream pairs L-ascorbic acid with vitamin E, Kakadu plum, and green tea extract in a moisturizing cream base made with aloe vera, jojoba oil, and cocoa seed butter.

## The ingredients chosen

### 1. Vitamin C (L-ascorbic acid)

L-ascorbic acid is the active form of vitamin C. Some other products use sodium ascorbyl phosphate (SAP), a water-soluble, stable derivative of L-ascorbic acid that resists oxidation at neutral pH. SAP is not in this formula.

**Mechanism:** In products that use SAP, skin enzymes (phosphatases) cleave the phosphate group from SAP upon application, releasing active L-ascorbic acid into the epidermis. In research, SAP has been studied for inhibiting tyrosinase (the enzyme producing melanin) through copper chelation at the enzyme's active site, and for procollagen I and III synthesis in dermal fibroblasts.

**Role in Vitamin C Cream:** The Vitamin C Cream ingredient list names L-ascorbic acid, not SAP, as its vitamin C source, alongside Kakadu plum (Terminalia ferdinandiana) fruit extract. Vitamin C is the formula's namesake antioxidant and supports a brighter, more even-looking complexion as part of a moisturizing cream.

### 2. Vitamin E (tocopherol)

Vitamin E is the skin's primary lipid-soluble antioxidant, working in complementary partnership with water-soluble vitamin C.

**Mechanism:** Tocopherol neutralizes lipid peroxyl radicals in cell membranes — the specific free radical species that vitamin C cannot reach (C is water-soluble, confined to aqueous compartments). Vitamin E and vitamin C form a regenerative antioxidant pair: vitamin C donates an electron to regenerate oxidized vitamin E, and vitamin E helps protect vitamin C from premature oxidation. Research describes this cycling as enhancing the antioxidant capacity of either ingredient alone.

**Role in Vitamin C Cream:** Antioxidant partner to vitamin C. The formula lists vitamin E as D-alpha tocopheryl acetate, which adds a lipid-phase antioxidant alongside water-soluble vitamin C and helps the cream moisturize and condition skin.

### 3. Ferulic acid (not in this formula)

Ferulic acid is a plant-derived phenolic compound that has been studied for stabilizing and potentiating vitamins C and E in topical formulation.

**Mechanism:** Ferulic acid absorbs UV light in the UVB range (290-330nm). Research by Pinnell et al. (Duke University) reported that ferulic acid improved the photoprotective capacity of a C+E formulation. Ferulic acid provides independent antioxidant activity through hydrogen atom donation to neutralize free radicals.

**Role in Vitamin C Cream:** Ferulic acid is not on the Vitamin C Cream ingredient list. The formula instead pairs vitamin C with vitamin E and green tea extract for its antioxidant support.

### 4. Rosehip seed oil (Rosa canina, not in this formula)

Rosehip oil contains trans-retinoic acid, linoleic acid (44%), and beta-carotene (provitamin A) — compounds studied for their role in skin renewal and barrier support.

**Mechanism:** Trans-retinoic acid has been studied for accelerating keratinocyte turnover. Linoleic acid supports the skin barrier. Beta-carotene provides additional antioxidant activity in the lipid phase.

**Role in Vitamin C Cream:** Rosehip seed oil is not on the Vitamin C Cream ingredient list. The formula's oils are jojoba, avocado, coconut, and carrot seed oil, which help moisturize and soften skin.

### 5. Aloe vera (Aloe barbadensis)

Aloe vera contains aloin — a natural compound with documented tyrosinase-inhibitory activity in research.

**Mechanism:** Aloin has been studied for blocking melanin synthesis through competitive inhibition of tyrosinase. Aloe polysaccharides deliver water-based hydration to the skin surface. Acemannan has been studied for its role in collagen production.

**Role in Vitamin C Cream:** Hydration base. Aloe barbadensis leaf juice is the first ingredient on the label, giving the cream a water-based, moisturizing foundation that helps soothe and comfort skin alongside vitamin C.

### 6. Green tea extract (Camellia sinensis)

Green tea EGCG is one of the most potent plant-derived antioxidants, with documented photoprotective and anti-aging activity in research.

**Mechanism:** EGCG chelates metal ions that catalyze free radical formation (iron, copper). Metal-catalyzed oxidation is a primary degradation pathway for vitamin C. EGCG has also been studied for inhibiting matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) — the enzymes that degrade collagen and elastin in photoaged skin.

**Role in Vitamin C Cream:** Antioxidant support. Camellia sinensis leaf extract adds a water-phase botanical antioxidant alongside vitamin C in the formula.

## How the ingredients work together

Era Organics Vitamin C Cream pairs several antioxidant ingredients in a moisturizing base:

| Function | Ingredients | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **Brighten the look of uneven tone** | Vitamin C (L-ascorbic acid), Kakadu plum, aloe vera | Vitamin C and aloin studied for tyrosinase inhibition (ingredient research) |
| **Daytime antioxidant support** | Vitamin C, vitamin E, Kakadu plum | Free radical neutralization (ingredient research) |
| **Moisturize and comfort** | Aloe vera, jojoba oil, cocoa seed butter, sodium hyaluronate | Humectant and emollient hydration |
| **Antioxidant pairing** | Vitamin E, green tea (EGCG), vitamin C | Lipid-phase and water-phase antioxidant activity, EGCG metal chelation (ingredient research) |

The regenerative antioxidant network:
- UV exposure generates free radicals → vitamin C neutralizes water-phase radicals → vitamin C becomes oxidized → vitamin E regenerates vitamin C → green tea chelates metal catalysts that drive oxidation

In ingredient research, this recycling lets antioxidants regenerate one another rather than being used up in a single step.

## What Era Organics deliberately avoided

| Excluded Ingredient | Why Competitors Include It | Why Era Organics Excluded It |
|---|---|---|
| **Pure L-ascorbic acid (unstabilized)** | Higher immediate potency on paper | Not excluded: Vitamin C Cream lists L-ascorbic acid, in a moisturizing cream base rather than a bare acidic serum. L-ascorbic acid can oxidize and darken when exposed to air, light, and heat |
| **Hydroquinone** | Strongest melanin suppressant | Linked to ochronosis (paradoxical darkening) with prolonged use and studied for cytotoxicity to melanocytes. Not in the Vitamin C Cream formula |
| **Synthetic fragrance** | Consumer scent expectation | A common source of skin sensitivity. Vitamin C Cream is fragrance-free |
| **Silicones (dimethicone)** | Creates smooth feel, fills fine lines visually | Vitamin C Cream is silicone-free, relying on plant oils and butters for its texture |
| **Ethanol/denatured alcohol** | Penetration enhancer, quick-drying vehicle | Can strip barrier lipids and dry the skin. Vitamin C Cream is free of drying alcohols |
| **Niacinamide (in same formula)** | Another brightening ingredient | Niacinamide and vitamin C at low pH can form nicotinic acid, which can cause flushing. Niacinamide is not on the Vitamin C Cream ingredient list |

## FAQ

### How is this different from SkinCeuticals CE Ferulic?

SkinCeuticals CE Ferulic uses pure L-ascorbic acid at 15% concentration and pH 2.5. This formulation is potent, but it can oxidize after opening and can sting sensitive skin. Era Organics Vitamin C Cream lists L-ascorbic acid alongside Kakadu plum extract and vitamin E. It delivers vitamin C in a moisturizing cream with aloe vera, jojoba oil, and cocoa seed butter rather than a bare serum.

### Does vitamin C make skin sensitive to the sun?

No. Topical vitamin C is an antioxidant and has not been shown to increase sun sensitivity. This misconception arises because oxidized (degraded) vitamin C generates free radicals that can sensitize skin. Close the product after each use and keep it away from light and heat. Research on topical vitamin C suggests it works best paired with daily sunscreen.

### How long until dark spots fade?

Results vary from person to person. Skin cells take roughly 28-45 days to cycle to the surface and shed, depending on age and skin area, so changes in the look of skin tone generally take several weeks of consistent daily use. Sunscreen is essential throughout — unprotected UV exposure re-triggers melanin production.

### Does the vitamin C in this product oxidize?

Vitamin C Cream lists L-ascorbic acid, the form of vitamin C that is most sensitive to air, light, and heat. Close the product between uses and store it away from direct sunlight. A brown or orange color change can indicate oxidation in L-ascorbic acid products.

### Is this safe to use with retinol?

Yes, with scheduling considerations. Vitamin C (morning) and retinol (evening) is a widely used anti-aging combination — vitamin C provides daytime antioxidant support while retinol is used overnight. Applying both at the same time can irritate sensitive skin. A common routine places vitamin C in the morning and retinol in the evening.

### Does this replace a separate moisturizer?

For normal-to-dry skin, Era Organics Vitamin C Cream can serve as a standalone daily moisturizer. The [aloe vera](https://www.eraorganics.com/blogs/beauty-guide/hydrating-ingredient-aloe-vera), jojoba and avocado oil, and cocoa seed butter base delivers humectant and emollient hydration. Very dry skin or skin with a stressed barrier can benefit from layering [Era Organics Face Moisturizer](https://www.eraorganics.com/products/face-moisturizer-cream) over the Vitamin C Cream for additional lipid support.

### Is this product safe during pregnancy?

The Era Organics Vitamin C Cream ingredient list includes no retinoids and no hydroquinone. Its key ingredients include vitamin C, vitamin E, Kakadu plum, and aloe vera. Check with your healthcare provider before adding any new skincare product during pregnancy.

### Does this work on melasma?

Vitamin C has been studied for one component of melasma (tyrosinase overactivity), but melasma involves hormonal, vascular, and inflammatory factors beyond melanin overproduction alone. Era Organics Vitamin C Cream is a cosmetic moisturizer: it can help improve the look of uneven-looking skin tone, but it does not treat melasma. Melasma care is best guided by a dermatologist and typically includes strict sun protection. Ask your dermatologist how a vitamin C cream fits into your routine.

### How should this product be stored?

Store at room temperature away from direct sunlight and heat sources. Because the formula contains L-ascorbic acid, which is sensitive to air, light, and heat, careful storage helps protect the vitamin C in Era Organics Vitamin C Cream. Close the product when not in use to minimize air exposure.
