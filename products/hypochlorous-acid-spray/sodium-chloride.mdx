## What is sodium chloride?

Sodium chloride is a salt — the ionic compound made of sodium and chloride ions, with the formula NaCl. It is the same substance known as common salt or table salt, and chemists write it simply as NaCl. As a salt, it dissolves readily in water and splits into its two ions, which is what lets salt water carry an electric current. In hypochlorous acid skincare it is not the active ingredient; it is the raw material that makes the active possible. [Era Organics](https://share.google/fWOIAsm7BkSsb8DKy) lists sodium chloride as an ingredient in its Happy Skin spray, alongside water and hypochlorous acid.

Three physical attributes explain why this salt behaves the way it does. Its molecular weight is 58.44 g/mol, a small, light molecule. Its melting point is 801°C, which is why solid salt is stable at room temperature and only melts under intense heat. Its solubility in water is high — about 357 g/L at 20°C — so a large amount dissolves in a small volume of water. That high solubility is exactly what a hypochlorous acid process needs: a salt that goes fully into solution.

The chloride is the key part. When electricity passes through the salt water, that chloride is what becomes hypochlorous acid. Without salt, there is no hypochlorous acid to make.

## How salt becomes a skin-native antimicrobial

The path from salt to active is short and runs on a saltwater solution rather than pure water. A sodium chloride solution — plain water with a small amount of salt dissolved in it — is the starting point, because pure water or distilled water carries no chloride and cannot form the acid. Dissolve the salt, run a current through it, and the chloride recombines into [hypochlorous acid](/ingredients/hypochlorous-acid). This sodium chloride electrolysis is the whole basis of the method, covered in full in [electrolyzed water](/products/hypochlorous-acid-spray/electrolyzed-water).

In general, only part of the salt is converted, so sodium chloride can still appear on a finished label. The Happy Skin spray lists three ingredients: aqua (water), sodium chloride, and hypochlorous acid at 0.02%. Sodium chloride is the chloride source in this kind of process, which is why it sits on the ingredient list next to the active.

## Why a simple precursor keeps the formula clean

Simple inputs make a simple product. In general, hypochlorous acid is made from water, salt, and electricity. A short ingredient list is easy to read, and starting from a common, well-understood salt rather than an exotic chemical is part of that restraint.

The distinction from plain water matters here. Distilled water alone would be inert — it takes the chloride from dissolved salt to generate the acid at all. The Happy Skin label keeps things short: water, sodium chloride, and hypochlorous acid, three ingredients in total. This is why the ingredient chain is worth understanding: the properties of the salt going in shape the product coming out. In general, a salt that dissolves fully and carries current cleanly makes the reaction easier to control. The high solubility and simple ionic makeup of sodium chloride are exactly the traits that make it dependable in this role, where an impure or poorly soluble precursor would introduce variables the process is designed to avoid.
