## Why Era Organics formulated this product

Breastfeeding nipple creams fall into three categories:

1. **Lanolin-based products** (Lansinoh, Medela PureLan): ultra-purified wool fat that creates an occlusive moisture barrier. Effective, but derived from sheep sebum (wool grease). Lanolin allergy affects 1.7-6.9% of the general population [SOURCE NEEDED: dermatitis contact allergy prevalence]. Allergic mothers may notice more nipple irritation.
2. **Multi-ingredient creams with phenoxyethanol**: some multi-ingredient nipple creams use phenoxyethanol as a preservative, and some mothers prefer to avoid it on skin their nursing infant may come into contact with.
3. **All-purpose nipple ointments (APNO)**: a compounded ointment containing mupirocin (antibiotic), betamethasone (steroid), and miconazole (antifungal). Typically prescribed by a healthcare provider and made up by a compounding pharmacy. Contains compounds not intended for infant ingestion.

Era Organics Organic Nipple Butter suits mothers who: (a) react to lanolin, (b) want a formula free of phenoxyethanol and parabens, and (c) prefer a plant-oil-and-beeswax balm over petroleum-based occlusion. Its full ingredient list: Chamomile Extract, Calendula Oil, Evening Primrose Oil, Sunflower Oil, Cocoa Butter, Beeswax, Apricot Oil, Vegetable Glycerin, Arrowroot Powder, Rosemary Extract, Mixed Tocopherols.

## Ingredients chosen and their mechanisms

### Calendula extract (Calendula officinalis)

Calendula contains triterpenoid saponins, flavonoids, and carotenoids that have been studied for wound-healing properties. The European Medicines Agency's herbal monograph on calendula lists it as a traditional herbal remedy for minor wounds and skin inflammation.

**Mechanism:** In studies, triterpenoid saponins (faradiol, arnidiol) stimulate granulation tissue formation, the regenerative tissue that fills wound beds. Flavonoids (quercetin, isorhamnetin) reduce pro-inflammatory cytokines. Calendula has increased fibroblast proliferation by 50-70% in vitro [SOURCE NEEDED: in vitro study citation].

**Role in formula:** Calendula oil is one of the formula's key actives. It adds a soothing, conditioning botanical oil that moisturizes nipple skin.

### Organic olive oil (Olea europaea)

Extra virgin olive oil provides oleic acid (72%), squalene, polyphenols (oleocanthal, hydroxytyrosol), and vitamin E. Olive oil has been used in wound care for over 5,000 years across Mediterranean medical traditions.

**Mechanism:** Oleic acid enhances skin permeability. Oleocanthal shows ibuprofen-like anti-inflammatory activity (identified in a 2005 Nature study). Squalene is a direct precursor to skin lipid synthesis.

**Role in formula:** Olive oil is not on Organic Nipple Butter's ingredient list. The formula's carrier oils are sunflower oil, apricot oil, and evening primrose oil, which moisturize and condition the skin.

### Beeswax (Cera alba)

Beeswax forms a semi-occlusive, breathable layer that helps protect the skin from friction and moisture loss. Unlike petroleum, beeswax is not fully occlusive.

**Mechanism:** Beeswax can form a light layer over the skin that may: (a) ease fabric friction, (b) help hold in moisture and plant oils, and (c) stay breathable rather than sealing the skin completely. Beeswax naturally contains vitamin A esters, which have been studied for their role in skin cell renewal.

**Role in formula:** Protective layer and formula structure. Gives the balm its texture and helps protect the skin barrier from friction.

### Organic coconut oil (Cocos nucifera)

Virgin coconut oil provides lauric acid (47%), the same medium-chain fatty acid that makes up 6.2% of human breast milk fat. Coconut oil has been studied for antimicrobial properties.

**Mechanism:** In laboratory studies, lauric acid disrupts bacterial cell membranes. Coconut oil's triglyceride structure absorbs readily into the skin and delivers moisture.

**Role in formula:** Coconut oil is not on Organic Nipple Butter's ingredient list. The formula's emollients include cocoa butter and sunflower oil, which moisturize and condition nipple skin.

### Shea butter (Vitellaria paradoxa)

Shea butter contains allantoin, cinnamic acid esters, and vitamins A, E, and F. Shea butter is edible and used in food preparation across West Africa.

**Mechanism:** Allantoin has been studied for promoting cell turnover. Cinnamic acid esters have been studied for inhibiting leukotriene synthesis (an inflammation pathway). The high stearic and oleic acid content provides emollient conditioning that softens dry skin.

**Role in formula:** Shea butter is not on Organic Nipple Butter's ingredient list. The butter in this formula is cocoa butter, which moisturizes and softens dry skin.

## How the ingredients work together

Nipple care during breastfeeding faces a unique challenge: the skin goes through latch friction every 2-3 hours during nursing. The formula is meant to moisturize between feedings and help protect the skin barrier during feedings.

| Phase | Ingredients | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Between feedings (moisturizing)** | Calendula oil, sunflower oil, evening primrose oil, apricot oil | Moisturizes and soothes dry, sore-feeling skin |
| **During feeding (protection)** | Beeswax lattice, cocoa butter | Helps protect the skin barrier from latch friction |
| **Post-feeding (recovery)** | Full formula reapplication | Restores moisture and the protective layer for the next feeding |

All ingredients work on a repeating cycle: moisturize, protect, reapply. The beeswax layer helps keep moisture in place during the friction of infant latch.

## How to use

### Standard application

1. **After each feeding or pumping session**: apply a pea-sized amount to the entire nipple and areola
2. **Wiping before the next feeding is up to you**: ask your lactation consultant or pediatrician if you're unsure
3. **Reapply after showering** or any time product has been removed
4. **Apply to breast pump flanges** (thin layer) for a smoother feel during pumping

### For severe cracks (bleeding fissures)

1. **Express a drop of breast milk** onto the crack after feeding (breast milk contains epidermal growth factor)
2. **Apply a generous layer** of nipple cream over the breast milk
3. **Use a non-stick breast pad** (not cotton, which sticks to broken skin) between feedings
4. **Reapply every 2 hours** even if not nursing, to keep the skin moisturized
5. **Ask your provider about short-term nipple shield use** for 24-48 hours to rest the deepest cracks before direct-latch exposure

### Duration of use

Continue application throughout breastfeeding or until nipple skin fully adapts to nursing (typically 2-4 weeks for first-time mothers). Resume use during teething phases, when infant bite pressure can irritate the skin again.

## FAQ

### Does this cream need to be removed before breastfeeding?

Era Organics Nipple Cream's ingredients are Chamomile Extract, Calendula Oil, Evening Primrose Oil, Sunflower Oil, Cocoa Butter, Beeswax, Apricot Oil, Vegetable Glycerin, Arrowroot Powder, Rosemary Extract, and Mixed Tocopherols. It is fragrance-free, paraben-free, and phenoxyethanol-free. If you're unsure whether to wipe before nursing, ask your lactation consultant or pediatrician.

### How does this compare to Lansinoh?

Lansinoh is 100% ultra-purified lanolin, a single-ingredient occlusive derived from sheep wool. Era Organics Nipple Cream is a multi-ingredient formula that combines moisturizing plant oils (calendula, sunflower, apricot, evening primrose) with a protective layer of beeswax and cocoa butter. Lansinoh creates a moisture barrier; Era Organics moisturizes with plant oils while also helping protect the skin barrier. Mothers with lanolin allergy (1.7-6.9% of population) may react to Lansinoh.

### Is lanolin allergy common enough to matter?

Contact allergy to lanolin affects 1.7-6.9% of the general population based on patch testing studies [SOURCE NEEDED: exact study citations]. Women with eczema-prone skin may be more likely to react to lanolin. Some mothers only discover a lanolin sensitivity after applying it to already-sore nipples, and can mistake the reaction for ordinary nursing soreness.

### Can this product be used before nipple soreness starts?

Applying before breastfeeding is established (the first latch) moisturizes nipple skin from the start. Consistent application between feedings from day one keeps the skin moisturized and comfortable. Proper infant latch technique remains the main way to avoid damage, and no topical product makes up for a poor latch.

### Does this cream affect infant feeding behavior?

Plant-based oils and beeswax are generally mild in taste and smell. Synthetic fragrances and petroleum alter taste and smell, potentially causing infant feeding refusal. Era Organics Nipple Cream is fragrance-free and contains no synthetic dyes.

### Is this product vegan?

No. Era Organics Nipple Cream contains beeswax (an insect-derived product). Lanolin is not on its ingredient list, but the beeswax means the formula is not vegan. Beeswax gives the balm its structure and texture.

### Can this cream help with thrush on nipples?

Nipple thrush (Candida) is usually treated with an antifungal prescribed by a provider (such as nystatin or gentian violet). Era Organics Nipple Cream is a moisturizing balm. It is not an antifungal and does not replace medical treatment for diagnosed thrush.

### How quickly do nipple cracks heal with this product?

Era Organics Nipple Cream is a balm that moisturizes and soothes. It is not a treatment for cracks, so it has no healing timeline. How quickly cracks improve depends on latch quality, feeding frequency, and crack depth. For cracks that bleed or don't improve, see a lactation consultant or healthcare provider.

### Does this product expire?

Check the packaging for date information. Store at room temperature away from direct sunlight. Discard if color, texture, or scent changes significantly.

### Can this product be used during pregnancy to prepare nipples?

Applying during the third trimester moisturizes nipple skin before breastfeeding begins. Apply gently and avoid stimulating the nipples, which can trigger contractions in late pregnancy, to keep the skin soft and moisturized. Consult a midwife or OB about nipple stimulation precautions after 36 weeks.
