## The ingredients chosen

### 1. Aloe vera (Aloe barbadensis)

Aloe vera gel consists of 99.5% water with polysaccharides (acemannan), amino acids, vitamins, and enzymes suspended in a gel matrix.

**Mechanism:** Aloe polysaccharides such as acemannan form a hydrating film on the skin surface. Amino acids in aloe (proline, hydroxyproline) are among the building blocks of skin proteins. The gel matrix delivers hydration without lipid addition — useful in a layered formula where lipids come from other ingredients.

**Role in Face Moisturizer:** Primary humectant. Helps attract and hold water in the skin's outer layers so skin feels moisturized.

### 2. Jojoba oil (Simmondsia chinensis)

Jojoba oil is a liquid wax ester structurally similar to human sebum. The wax ester profile resembles the skin's own surface lipids.

**Mechanism:** Jojoba wax esters blend into the skin's surface lipid layer, softening and smoothing dry areas. The structural similarity to human sebum is one reason jojoba is widely used in sensitive-skin care. Jojoba has also been studied for its behavior alongside the skin's natural sebum.

**Role in Face Moisturizer:** Emollient. Helps soften skin and support the skin's lipid layer.

### 3. Shea butter (Vitellaria paradoxa)

Shea butter delivers cinnamic acid esters, triterpenes (lupeol, amyrin), and a fatty acid profile dominated by oleic (46%) and stearic (43%) acids.

**Mechanism:** Stearic acid is one of the fatty acids found in the skin's own lipid matrix. Cinnamic acid esters and lupeol in shea have been studied in laboratory research for their soothing properties.

**Role in Face Moisturizer:** Emollient and comfort. Helps soothe and comfort sensitive skin while helping protect the skin barrier.

### 4. Cocoa butter (Theobroma cacao)

Cocoa butter provides saturated fatty acids (stearic 33%, palmitic 25%) and polyphenolic antioxidants (flavanols, catechins, epicatechins).

**Mechanism:** The saturated fatty acid profile creates a stable layer that resists oxidation and rancidity. Cocoa polyphenols are antioxidants. The melting point near skin temperature (34-38°C) means the butter softens on contact and spreads into a thin film.

**Role in Face Moisturizer:** Moisture seal. Helps reduce transepidermal water loss (TEWL) so skin stays feeling moisturized.

### 5. Rosehip seed oil (Rosa canina)

Rosehip seed oil is a plant oil rich in linoleic acid (44%) and alpha-linolenic acid (33%), and it is common in facial oils.

**Note:** Rosehip seed oil is not in the Era Organics Face Moisturizer ingredient list.

**Role in Face Moisturizer:** None — the formula does not contain it. For plant lipids, the formula relies on shea butter, cocoa butter, coconut oil, and olive oil instead.

### 6. Vitamin E (tocopherol)

Vitamin E is the skin's primary lipid-soluble antioxidant — naturally present in sebum and the stratum corneum lipid matrix.

**Mechanism:** Tocopherol intercepts lipid peroxidation chain reactions. Oxidized lipids lose their softening quality — vitamin E helps slow this. Tocopherol also works alongside other antioxidants such as vitamin C.

**Role in Face Moisturizer:** Antioxidant ingredient (as tocopheryl acetate), included alongside the formula's oils and butters.

### 7. Chamomile extract (Matricaria chamomilla)

Chamomile contains bisabolol and chamazulene, and it appears in many sensitive-skin products.

**Note:** Chamomile is not in the Era Organics Face Moisturizer ingredient list.

**Role in Face Moisturizer:** None — the formula does not contain it. Soothing comfort in this formula comes from aloe vera, oat kernel flour, and shea butter.

## How the ingredients work together

Era Organics Face Moisturizer works through a three-layer hydration approach:

| Hydration Layer | Ingredients | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Attract water** (humectant) | Aloe vera | Draws moisture into the skin's outer layers |
| **Fill barrier gaps** (emollient) | Jojoba oil, coconut oil, shea butter | Soften skin and support the skin's lipid layer |
| **Seal moisture** (occlusive) | Cocoa butter, shea butter | Help reduce TEWL through a surface film, lock in humectant-attracted water |

Cross-ingredient synergies:
- Aloe vera attracts water → shea butter and coconut oil help hold that water in the skin's surface → cocoa butter helps seal the surface against evaporation
- Shea butter and aloe vera help soothe and comfort sensitive skin → the lipids from shea and cocoa butter help protect the skin barrier
- Vitamin E (tocopheryl acetate) is included as an antioxidant alongside the oils and butters in the formula (shea, cocoa, coconut, olive)
- Sodium hyaluronate (hyaluronic acid) and glycerin add more water-binding humectants alongside aloe vera

## How to use Face Moisturizer

### Morning routine

1. Gentle non-sulfate cleanser (or water-only rinse for very dry/sensitive skin)
2. Facial mist or toner (optional)
3. Water-based serum (hyaluronic acid, niacinamide) — if using
4. Era Organics Face Moisturizer — apply to slightly damp skin for maximum humectant effect
5. Mineral sunscreen SPF 30+

### Evening routine

1. Oil cleanser or micellar water (remove sunscreen/makeup)
2. Gentle non-sulfate cleanser
3. Active serums (retinol, vitamin C) — if using
4. Era Organics Face Moisturizer — apply generous layer as final step to lock in moisture

### Application technique

Dispense a nickel-sized amount. Dot onto forehead, cheeks, nose, and chin. Press into skin with flat palms — pressing is gentler than rubbing, which creates friction on sensitive skin.

### Key principle: apply to damp skin

Aloe vera's humectant action captures surface water. Applying to dry skin limits the water available for this. Mist face with water or apply within 60 seconds of rinsing for maximum hydration capture.
