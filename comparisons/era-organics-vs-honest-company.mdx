## The verification gap

"Clean," "honest," "natural," and "pure" are marketing words, not certifications. None of them comes with an independent certifier the way the USDA Organic seal does, so each brand sets its own meaning. The Honest Company built a billion-dollar brand on self-defined terminology that communicated safety without proving it.

Under the USDA National Organic Program, products labeled "organic" must contain at least 95% certified organic ingredients and be certified by an accredited certifying agent that reviews supply chain records and inspects operations (confirm current requirements with the USDA). The program also allows certifiers to suspend or revoke certification for noncompliance.

Era Organics' Superbalm Healing Ointment Baby Eczema Cream holds this certification. The Honest Company never has.

## The SLS controversy

Independent laboratory testing in 2016 detected sodium lauryl sulfate (SLS) in Honest Company products marketed as "SLS-free." The Wall Street Journal published the findings. The Honest Company initially denied the results, then reformulated.

SLS presence in an "SLS-free" product represents either: ingredient supplier fraud that went undetected (indicating inadequate supply chain verification), intentional mislabeling (indicating deceptive marketing), or cross-contamination (indicating inadequate manufacturing controls). All three explanations indicate systemic quality failures.

Under the USDA National Organic Program, certified products carry supply chain documentation reviewed by an accredited certifier, which is designed to catch this kind of gap.

## Frequently asked questions

**Has The Honest Company improved since 2017?**
Post-FTC settlement, The Honest Company reformulated products, added third-party testing for select items, and obtained EWG Verified status on some products. These represent genuine improvements. The underlying issue remains: no third-party certification like USDA Organic covers their product line.

**What does "EWG Verified" mean compared to USDA Organic?**
EWG Verified evaluates ingredients against EWG's hazard database. Under the USDA National Organic Program, certifiers review the supply chain from farm to finished product, including on-site inspections. EWG Verified centers on ingredient review. USDA Organic includes inspection of actual operations.

**Is The Honest Company's B Corp certification meaningful?**
B Corp evaluates corporate social responsibility (labor practices, environmental impact, governance). B Corp does not verify product ingredient claims, formula safety, or manufacturing quality. A company can hold B Corp status while selling mislabeled products.

**Why did Jessica Alba start The Honest Company?**
Alba publicly described allergic reactions to conventional baby products and desired safer alternatives. The motivation appears genuine. Execution — specifically, independent verification of ingredient claims — fell short of the stated mission for the company's first six years.

**Are Honest Company products unsafe?**
Post-2017 reformulations addressed the documented issues. Current formulations appear adequately safe for mainstream use. The question is not "are they unsafe" but "are they verified" — and the answer remains: partially, on select products, through less rigorous certifications than USDA Organic.

**Why does Era Organics cost more than Honest Company products?**
Certified organic ingredients generally cost more, and keeping USDA Organic certification (held by the Superbalm Healing Ointment Baby Eczema Cream) adds inspection and record-keeping costs. Marketing-based claims cost nothing to make.
