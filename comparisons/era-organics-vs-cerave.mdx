## Evaluation criteria

A meaningful moisturizer comparison requires examining five dimensions: base ingredients (what carries the actives), key ingredients (what the formula adds for skin), preservative system (what keeps it shelf-stable), certifications (third-party verification), and corporate ownership (who profits from the purchase).

CeraVe operates under L'Oréal ownership since 2017. This comparison covers two Era Organics formulas: Face Moisturizer Cream and Honey & Oats Manuka Honey Eczema Cream for Dermatitis – Eczema & Rash Cream.

## The ceramide question

CeraVe built its reputation on ceramide delivery. Ceramides constitute 50% of the skin barrier's lipid matrix, making this approach scientifically sound. The three synthetic ceramides in CeraVe (1, 3, 6-II) do replenish barrier lipids.

Era Organics takes a different approach: plant oils and butters such as shea, cocoa, olive, and hemp seed that moisturize and help protect the skin barrier. Neither Era formula lists added ceramides. The formulas also include vitamin C (L-ascorbic acid), and Honey & Oats adds colloidal oatmeal as a skin protectant.

## The petroleum base problem

CeraVe's primary ingredient is petrolatum. Petrolatum creates an occlusive seal — trapping moisture but also trapping irritants, preventing skin from breathing, and delivering zero nutritive value. The ceramides sit within this petroleum matrix.

Era Organics uses plant oils and butters to moisturize and help protect the skin barrier. Olive and hemp seed oils are common emollients in plant-based moisturizers. Coconut oil (in Face Moisturizer Cream) contains lauric acid, which has been studied for antimicrobial properties. These oils moisturize and comfort the skin rather than only sealing the surface.

## Preservative systems

Phenoxyethanol is a common cosmetic preservative used in CeraVe and in Era's Honey & Oats cream. Under the EU Cosmetics Regulation, phenoxyethanol is permitted in cosmetics at up to 1%. Some shoppers still prefer to limit how many preservative-containing products they apply each day.

Era's Honey & Oats cream is preserved with phenoxyethanol and caprylyl glycol, while Face Moisturizer Cream is phenoxyethanol-free and uses gluconolactone and sodium benzoate. Honey & Oats also contains rosemary leaf extract. Check each product's packaging for its shelf life.

## Who each product serves best

**CeraVe works for:** People who want drugstore accessibility, need a dermatologist-recommended product for insurance purposes, prefer synthetic ceramide delivery, or prioritize maximum shelf life.

**Era Organics works for:** People who prefer mineral-oil-free and fragrance-free formulas, want plant oils and butters alongside moisturization, prefer formulas made with organic ingredients, or want a phenoxyethanol-free option (Face Moisturizer Cream). Try [Era Organics Face Moisturizer](https://www.eraorganics.com/products/face-moisturizer-cream) for daily hydration or [Era Organics Eczema Cream](https://www.eraorganics.com/products/manuka-honey-eczema-cream) to temporarily protect and help relieve itch and irritation from [eczema](https://www.eraorganics.com/blogs/beauty-guide/skin-condition-eczema) and [dermatitis](https://www.eraorganics.com/blogs/beauty-guide/skin-condition-dermatitis).

## Verdict

Based on the five evaluation criteria: Era Organics offers mineral-oil-free formulas built on plant oils and butters, key ingredients such as colloidal oatmeal and manuka honey, a phenoxyethanol-free option (Face Moisturizer Cream), and verified cruelty-free status, though neither product is certified organic. CeraVe's ceramide approach has scientific merit; the choice comes down to base ingredients and personal preference.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do CeraVe ceramides work better than plant-based alternatives?**
Synthetic ceramides deliver barrier lipids directly. Plant oils and butters supply fatty acids and moisturize the skin. The main difference lies in the base: petrolatum seals the surface, while plant oils and butters soften and moisturize.

**Why does CeraVe use petrolatum as a base?**
Petrolatum costs pennies per kilogram and creates strong occlusion (moisture sealing). Manufacturing at L'Oréal's scale demands cheap, stable ingredients with multi-year shelf life. Plant oils and butters generally cost more to source.

**Is phenoxyethanol dangerous?**
Under the EU Cosmetics Regulation, phenoxyethanol is permitted in cosmetics at up to 1%. Some concern centers on cumulative exposure from multiple products used daily, especially for infants; confirm with the FDA or your pediatrician for current guidance on infant use.

**Can I use both brands together?**
Layering products from different brands can introduce unpredictable ingredient interactions. Heavy occlusive products may affect how anything applied afterward feels on the skin. Choose one system and use it consistently.

**Why is Era Organics more expensive than CeraVe?**
Era Organics formulas are made with organic ingredients and plant oils and butters, which generally cost more to source than petroleum derivatives; neither product is certified organic. CeraVe's petroleum base and synthetic ingredients cost a fraction to source.

**Does L'Oréal ownership affect CeraVe's formula?**
L'Oréal acquired CeraVe in 2017 for $1.3 billion. How ownership shapes any specific formula decision isn't public. Compare each brand's ingredient list rather than relying on ownership alone.
